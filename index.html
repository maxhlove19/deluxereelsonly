How it works

A clear brief.
A focused edit.

The project starts with the source, the audience, and the deliverables—not a blanket package. We agree the output mix, review steps, and schedule before editing begins.

01

You share the brief

Send the source format, target channels, desired deliverables, reference style, and timing needs. Raw phone video, a podcast, an interview, an event, or a livestream can all be scoped.

02

I shape the edit

Claude, Python, FFmpeg, and Whisper help with transcript review, clip preparation, captions, and repeatable production tasks. Human creative direction guides the hook, story, pacing, framing, brand fit, and quality check.

03

We review and deliver

You review against the agreed direction. Approved versions are prepared to the agreed channel, caption, audio, framing, and export specifications.

How scope gets set

Clear before the first cut.

  • Source: format, length, condition, and handoff method.
  • Deliverables: output mix, channels, captions, audio, framing, and exports.
  • Creative: audience, references, brand direction, and approval criteria.
  • Plan: review steps, capacity, dependencies, and schedule.

Share the brief in a direct message. After the material and goals are reviewed, you'll receive a recommended scope and next steps.

Questions

The real ones.

How are projects priced?

After I review the source, channels, deliverables, creative direction, and review needs. The proposal sets the scope and terms before editing begins; capacity and outputs are defined rather than left open-ended.

What affects the schedule?

Source length and condition, the number and format of deliverables, edit complexity, dependencies, and review needs. Timing is confirmed as part of the scope rather than promised before the footage is reviewed.

What can you make from long-form footage?

Focused short-form cuts with hooks, pacing, captions, audio cleanup, graphics where they serve the idea, platform-specific reframing, and approved export versions. The exact mix is agreed for the project.

Do you write the hooks or do I?

I do, from what you actually said. I won't put words in your mouth — the hook gets built out of the strongest sentence already in the footage.

What do you need from me to start?

Your source format, target channels, desired deliverables, reference style, and timing needs. A footage link or representative sample helps me recommend the right scope.

Do I have to get on a call?

No. The inquiry and project details can start in writing. If a conversation would clarify a complex brief, we can decide that together.

How do the tools fit into the workflow?

Claude, Python, FFmpeg, and Whisper help accelerate transcript review, clip preparation, captions, and repeatable production work. They support the process; human creative direction and quality assurance decide what makes the final cut.

Is every reel client work?

No. The portfolio labels sample footage and real client footage separately. The edits are shown as evidence of the work itself, without presenting a sample subject as a client.

Who's actually doing the editing?

Max Love. The workflow uses purpose-built tools for repeatable production tasks, while creative direction, story judgment, and final quality assurance remain hands-on.
